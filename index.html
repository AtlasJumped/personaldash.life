Why this exists

Reasons people hesitate to adopt AI

These reasons apply at every level, from a first chat to a daily power user. Everyone is on a separate journey, and people learn the same lessons alone. We think everyone should reach a basic standard that is easy to reach.

You find out after years that you used it wrong

That is the most frustrating one. Files saved in the wrong places, lost context between chats, and money spent on the wrong plan. We learned these lessons the expensive way, so you do not have to.

I don't know how to use it

Most people open a chat box, type a question, and stop there. Nobody showed them what else it can do.

I don't know how to optimize it

Without a comparison to what other people are doing, you cannot tell whether you are doing something well or missing something simple others do.

No app shows how to optimize it

We do not know of an app that looks at how you use your AI and shows you how to use it better. This does, with your own assistant, and it says plainly what it cannot see. This tool takes you from 0 to 95 right away, and the rest is up to you.

No app shows your usage and models

It is hard to see your own usage, and which models you use for which jobs. The setup asks your assistant to list the accounts, connectors, and models you use and what you do most. If there is a way to automate which models are used, the tool just does it for you.

These problems plagued early adopters. The fix is simple: clear structure and instructions from day one. The simple solution to all of these problems is to copy and paste the text at the bottom of this page.

Everyone is lifted. Even people who have never felt at home with modern technology have reached a time when one document unlocks the powers of artificial intelligence that advanced users have been using, right at your fingertips.

The HMR written instructions

We set up the rules so you don't have to

AI can do so much that it is hard to know what rules to set. Engineers already set rules like these for their AI. If you haven't, take ours and start from here. Nobody who has never used AI should be scared of it.

  1. The company's rules.Whoever makes your AI sets these, nobody can change them, and your AI tells you plainly when one stops it.
  2. Our partnership rules.These are the HMR written instructions for human and AI interaction: the rules of engagement between you and your AI, ready as a starting point.
  3. Your own rules.Your notes and corrections can change ours, but your AI still asks before spending, sending, deleting, or anything permanent.

Works with any AI. These instructions are written for Claude, and the same rules carry over to ChatGPT or Gemini, though some steps depend on what that assistant can do.

Here is the middle layer, our nine rules:

  1. Just talk.Turn on your microphone and say whatever is on your mind, in any order, and your AI works out what you meant.
  2. Side notes.Say "side note" and your AI stops chatting, writes it down, and handles it.
  3. Nothing gets dropped.If you forget something your AI said, it does not call you out, and it brings it back later, one thing at a time.
  4. Short replies.Your AI leads with the answer and tells you when things are handled, only when they truly are.
  5. Front end, back end.You are the front end in the real world, and your AI is the back end that does the digital work and keeps track.
  6. Clear instructions get done.When you say it clearly, your AI does it without asking again.
  7. Your words win.What you say or type outranks anything your AI suggests or summarizes.
  8. Mistakes come to the front.When you say it slipped, your AI checks the record, tells you straight who was right, and notes the lesson if it was wrong.
  9. Teach me about this process.Say these words at any time and your AI explains the step you are on, in plain words.

Our secret weapon is five words: "teach me about this process." Say them at any time and your AI explains the step you are on in plain words, like having an expert in almost every field beside you. It can be wrong, so it tells you when it is unsure. All you do is talk: our rules have your AI keep track of what you say, in a saved file or a list in the chat, and bring it back later.

Fewer corrections. We tell AI what to do a lot, and correct it a lot. Ours does it the first time, and if you say it slipped, it checks the record and tells you straight who was right. If it was wrong, it says so, writes the lesson at the top of its notes, and says how it will avoid a repeat.

One rule never bends: spending money, sending anything in your name, deleting, or anything permanent still needs your yes.

This is how AI drives positive change: collaboration, cooperation, intention, a shared mission, and shared values.

Bringing humans closer

The HMR family of sites has a huge focus on family connections, and especially on the well-being of children moving forward. Here is how we see the problem. Everybody's model of reality changes a little bit. So what we think is real and not real, and important and not important, changes a little bit. Then the language changes a little bit. Eventually there is a disconnect between a grandparent, a parent, and a child.

That has been the status quo for humanity, and it's okay. But we can use AI to bridge that disconnect.
